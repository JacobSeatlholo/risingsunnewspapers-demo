Jobless youth question eThekwini's unemployment decrease

Young residents say the lived reality on the ground tells a different story than the latest positive numbers.

RS By Rising Sun NewsroomRising Sun Newspapers · 12 November 2026 · 4 min read

When the numbers and the street disagree

A positive headline about falling unemployment should have been good news for eThekwini's young people. Instead, it has become the latest flashpoint between official statistics and the day-to-day reality of job-seeking in communities like Chatsworth, Merebank and Montford.

On the ground, the questions come quickly: which jobs were counted, where are they, and who got them? For households where one income supports several people, the gap between a percentage point and a payslip is the whole story.

Youth unemployment has been one of the defining challenges facing South Africa's metros for more than a decade. When a municipality reports improvement, residents naturally want to see the evidence in their own neighbourhoods - in vacancy lists, in learnership intakes, in the queue outside the local employment office.

What young residents say they want

In conversations reflected in Rising Sun's coverage, the same themes keep returning:

  • Visibility - knowing which opportunities actually exist, and where to apply
  • Transport - getting to an interview should not cost a day's wage
  • Skills - short courses and learnerships that end in real contracts
  • Local first - municipal jobs and procurement that look to nearby communities

Each of these sits within the reach of local government, business and community organisations working together - which is precisely why readers keep holding the numbers to account.

Why local journalism matters here

National headlines move on within a news cycle. A community newspaper stays: it attends the community meetings, asks the ward councillors, and returns to the same young people a month later to ask what changed. That continuity is what turns a statistic back into a story.
