What's missing today

Evidence from the audit

Discoverability — search engines and AI assistants cannot read you properly

Critical
  • Homepage has no meta description meta-description: fail The Lighthouse SEO audit fails outright. Google and AI assistants fall back to arbitrary snippets, so your own description of the paper never appears.
  • robots.txt is invalid — 2 syntax errors robots-txt: 2 errors A malformed robots.txt means crawler directives may simply be ignored. You may be accidentally blocking (or failing to guide) both search and AI crawlers.
  • No llms.txt — no machine-readable site summary llms-txt: not found The Lighthouse agentic-browsing category reports llms.txt absent. AI assistants that look for a curated map of the site find nothing.
  • Images without alt attributes image-alt: fail The top-navigation icon image ships with no alt text — a free accessibility and SEO win currently missed.
  • Links without discernible names link-name: fail Image links to Highway Mail, Kempton Express, Pretoria Rekord, Caxton Network News and The Citizen have no accessible names — invisible to screen readers and to agents.
  • Low-resolution favicon used as a navigation icon image-size-responsive: fail The site serves a .ico file where a modern icon belongs — blurry rendering and an image-resolution audit failure.

Agentic browsing — the newest score: 49/100

Critical
  • Accessibility tree is not well-formed agent-accessibility-tree: 0 AI agents and screen readers both navigate via the accessibility tree. This audit scores 0 — the page is effectively unreadable to automated assistants.
  • Heading order breaks the document outline heading-order: fail The 'TOP STORIES' block skips heading levels, so the page structure agents rely on to summarise content collapses.
  • Colour contrast below WCAG AA color-contrast: fail 'Read More', 'All' and the network-title links fail contrast checks — text both humans and machines struggle to read reliably.
  • No WebMCP tools or agent manifests registered webmcp-*: not applicable The emerging WebMCP audits report nothing registered — the site offers no structured way for agents to interact with it (search, subscribe, verify).
  • No agent readiness (ARD) manifest ard-schema: not found No manifest declaring the site's agent-facing capabilities exists.

Performance — readers wait, agents time out

High
  • Time to Interactive: 15.7 seconds on mobile TTI 15,722ms · score 6 Lighthouse scores this 6/100. Readers on mid-range phones wait 15 seconds before the page truly responds; agents consider the page unresponsive.
  • Total Blocking Time 610ms · LCP 3.5s · Speed Index 4.2s TBT 606ms · LCP 3,453ms Heavy theme JavaScript (jQuery plus Jannah bundles) blocks the main thread; the largest element paints late.
  • DOM of 2,617 elements at 22 levels deep dom-size: 2,617 nodes Theme bloat inflates style and layout cost. A lean news article needs roughly a quarter of that.
  • Critical network chain of 2.48 seconds longest chain 2,478ms Render-blocking CSS files and a font dependency chain delay first paint — with unused preconnects to ad-tech origins wasting connection time.
  • Forced reflows and console errors from theme scripts forced-reflow: fail · console errors Layout-thrashing JavaScript and unresolved browser errors drag interactivity down further.
  • Unsized logo image shifts the layout cls-culprits: unsized img The header logo loads without reserved space and pushes content down as it paints — a core CLS culprit named in the report.

Trust, security & hygiene

Medium
  • No frame-control policy — clickjacking exposure rated High clickjacking: High severity Lighthouse finds neither X-Frame-Options nor a CSP frame-ancestors directive. Any site could embed yours and trick readers.
  • Third-party sprawl 5+ third-party origins Clicky analytics, Lotame (crwdcntrl) data brokers, onthe.io and consent tooling all add weight — with unused preconnects to ad-tech origins signalling past integrations still haunting the head.
  • Console errors unresolved errors-in-console: fail Browser errors logged on load indicate failing requests and scripts — a best-practices audit failure.

The AI discoverability layer

Be the answer, not the footnote

When residents ask an AI assistant “what happened in Chatsworth today?”, the assistant decides which source to cite. Sites that are fast, semantic, schema-rich and explicitly welcoming to AI crawlers get quoted - and get the click-through. Sites that aren't get paraphrased from third parties, or skipped.

  • 1robots.txt that speaks robot. Today's file has syntax errors; ours is valid and names GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended and friends with an explicit Allow.
  • 2llms.txt. A curated, structured summary of the paper - brand, editions, desks, policies - at the root of the site, ready for any assistant.
  • 3Structured data everywhere. NewsArticle, NewsMediaOrganization, BreadcrumbList and FAQPage JSON-LD, so agents can verify authorship, dates and publisher trust.
  • 4An accessibility tree agents can read. One h1 per page, ordered headings, named links and AA contrast - the exact audits scoring 0 today.
  • 5Human journalism, amplified. Aligned with your AI Policy: no AI-generated news - we make the humans' work machine-legible instead.

The roadmap

Ten weeks to a transformed site

Search & AI quick wins

Weeks 1–2

  • Meta descriptions on every template
  • Fix robots.txt; publish valid AI-crawler policy
  • Ship llms.txt and llms-full.txt
  • Alt text, link names, heading order, crisp favicon

Performance & platform cleanup

Governance

  • Screen-reader accessibility passes each quarter
  • Schema validation in the editorial CI

Questions you'll ask

Answered plainly
Will AI replace our journalists?

No — and your own AI Policy says so: Caxton Local Media does not use AI to generate news, and AI will never replace journalists or editors. Our work makes that human journalism legible to machines: agents quote your reporting accurately, attribute it properly and send readers (and new audiences) back to you.

What is 'agentic browsing'?

Assistants and AI browsers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and others) now fetch, summarise and act on web pages on a reader's behalf. Lighthouse 13 introduced an agentic-browsing category that scores how well a page serves those agents — this site currently scores 49/100. The demo shows exactly what that number wants.

Why does robots.txt matter so much?

When robots.txt is malformed, crawlers may ignore your instructions entirely — you lose control over what is and is not fetched. A valid file also lets you explicitly welcome AI assistants instead of leaving the decision to chance.

What is llms.txt?

An emerging convention: a short, structured file at the root of the site that tells large language models what the site is, what matters most and where to look. It is the fastest, cheapest AI-discoverability win available — the demo ships one.

Is this a full redesign of our website?

No. It is a demonstration of direction. Phases 1 and 2 of the roadmap deliberately work with your existing WordPress platform; a move to a faster stack is an option, not a requirement.

How will we know it worked?

A monthly scorecard: Lighthouse categories, Core Web Vitals from real users, search impressions from Google Search Console, and referral traffic from AI assistants. Targets we propose: Performance 95+, Accessibility 98+, SEO 100, Agentic Browsing 90+ on the new templates.
