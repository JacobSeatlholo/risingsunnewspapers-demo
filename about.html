Our story

Since 1986, the sun has risen on KwaZulu-Natal's communities

In 1986 there was a dire need for an affordable newspaper within the Indian community. Vijay Maharaj answered it - launching a free newspaper to document, profile and broadcast the latest news and advertising specials. Forty years later, the mission has not changed.

A newspaper built by its community

What began as one free sheet grew into a family of titles across south Durban, the mid and north coasts and Lenasia - each one written by journalists who live in, shop in and raise families in the neighbourhoods they cover. Today the Rising Sun is a product of Caxton Local Media, part of one of South Africa's oldest publishing houses, but the heartbeat is still unmistakably local.

  1. 1986

    Vijay Maharaj launches the Rising Sun - a free newspaper for a community the mainstream press too often overlooked.

  2. 1990s & 2000s

    Edition by edition, the paper grows across Chatsworth, Overport, Merebank, the mid and north coasts and Lenasia - always free to read, always local first.

  3. Today

    Seven community editions plus weekly e-papers, a 24/7 website, and a newsroom whose policy is explicit: journalism is written by humans, for humans.

  4. Next

    The same journalism, made discoverable to the next generation of readers - wherever they search, scroll or ask an AI assistant for the local story.
